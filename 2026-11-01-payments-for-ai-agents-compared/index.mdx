An AI agent needs one data lookup. The price is $0.001. Before the provider can return a result, the agent needs a way to pay that leaves the sale worth making.

We build Atto for small, direct payments: no protocol transfer fees, first-party MCP, CLI and n8n integrations, and public node access. Its wallet tools are available today; a complete paid-HTTP service still needs custom payment logic.

USDC brings dollar pricing and existing x402 support. Lightning serves bitcoin payments through L402. Nano offers another feeless option with community agent tools. The useful choice depends on the service, the payment size and the integration you want to run.

## Compare the payment you need to make

The fee comparison covers **$0.001 calls paid individually**. USDC scores use Coinbase's CDP Facilitator after its monthly free allowance; Atto and Nano use direct transfers, and Lightning uses funded channels.

Timings come from different network measurements and do not measure complete API purchases. Base uses L2 preconfirmation; Lightning uses off-chain settlement. For Atto and Nano, a confirmed send is enough to deliver the purchase; receiving it later makes the merchant's funds spendable.

## When a $1 workload leaves nothing to run the service

**1,000 calls at $0.001 each earn $1.** After CDP's monthly free allowance, one successful `exact` settlement per call costs **$0.001**, adding up to **$1 in fees and $0 left before serving the requests**. The first 1,000 monthly transactions are free, verification is free, and the fee already covers settlement gas. [CDP pricing](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/seller/facilitator), [pricing FAQ](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/support/faq).

Under that paid tariff, individually settled calls at or below $0.001 cannot cover service costs. Larger **mini payments** have more room: the same fee takes 10% of a $0.01 sale or 1% of a $0.10 sale. Batching or another facilitator changes the economics.

Atto and Nano charge no protocol transfer fee. Funding, conversion, computational work and infrastructure still cost resources. USDC makes dollar pricing easier, while adding issuer and redemption conditions. [USDC terms](https://www.circle.com/legal/usdc-terms).

## What each option offers

### Atto: feeless payments with first-party agent tools

Atto suits individual payments where a protocol fee would consume too much of the sale. The first-party [MCP](/build/mcp), [CLI](/build/cli) and [n8n](/build/n8n) tools use public services, so developers can start without finding or hosting a node.

The CLI/MCP wallet starts read-only and lets you approve [per-payment and rolling spending limits](/docs/integration/mcp#approve-a-spending-allowance) locally. Those packages are in beta. A complete paid-API flow still needs custom quoting, payment matching and delivery logic; the [x402-style flow and demonstration endpoint](/docs/roadmap#phase-7-micropayment-rails) remain planned work. ATTO has a floating dollar value.

### Nano: feeless payments with community integrations

Nano also fits small, direct payments. Its community [Feeless402 CLI/MCP](https://github.com/Feeless402/feeless402) provides public RPC defaults, and [Nano's agent page](https://nano.org/agents) links x402 tooling. Running your own node is optional.

Check confirmation handling before delivering a purchase: the linked x402 implementation can return a block hash before confirmation. Its [implementation notes](https://github.com/x402nano/exact#security-notes-) explain deployment limits. XNO also has a floating dollar value.

### USDC on Base: dollar pricing and existing x402 support

USDC on Base fits services that price in dollars and already support x402. The [client SDK](https://docs.x402.org/getting-started/quickstart-for-buyers) and hosted facilitator provide a paid-HTTP path without operating a Base node.

The CDP fee example above makes this setup better suited to larger small payments once the free allowance is exhausted. The table accepts an L2 preconfirmed receipt for delivery; a seller requiring stronger finality must wait longer.

### USDC on Solana: dollar payments for Solana services

USDC on Solana serves the same dollar-pricing need through another network. It is useful when the seller and agent already use [compatible Solana x402 tooling](https://docs.x402.org/core-concepts/network-and-token-support).

Configure the wallet and recipient token accounts for that path. Native transfers have network fees; a hosted facilitator can cover gas through its own tariff. The CDP per-settlement fee applies here too, so a fast network alone does not solve the $0.001 sale problem.

### Bitcoin Lightning: paid access through a Lightning wallet

Lightning fits bitcoin services using [L402](https://docs.lightning.engineering/the-lightning-network/l402/l402). Lightning Labs' [lnget CLI/MCP](https://github.com/lightninglabs/lnget) can pay a challenge and retry the request; [Alby MCP](https://github.com/getAlby/mcp/) connects agents and n8n workflows to a compatible wallet.

A wallet provider can remove the need to run your own node. Self-hosting adds channel and liquidity management; routing and provider fees vary. Bitcoin's dollar value moves with the market.

## Start with the purchase

Choose a payment option the service accepts, then test a small purchase and its timeout recovery. A prepaid service balance may also be simpler when calls can be metered internally.

To try Atto, start with the [integration guides](/docs/integration), fund a small wallet through the [faucet](/faucet), and test payment, confirmation and delivery together.

<summary>Rating rules, measurement sources and implementation notes</summary>

**Comparison scope.** The economics scores compare individual $0.001 payments: direct Atto/Nano transfers, CDP `exact` settlements after the monthly free allowance on Base/Solana, and Lightning payments through funded channels. Economics scores are 5 for no protocol transfer fee, 3 for route-dependent costs and 1 when the stated gateway fee consumes the sale. They are not total operating-cost estimates or production-reliability ratings.

**Agent integration.** A 5 means a packaged agent interface or SDK has a documented public or hosted backend path, without operating a node or writing a custom adapter. All five options have such a path. Lower scores would indicate more custom code or infrastructure work. This assesses agent access rather than paid-API readiness, reliability or identical setup effort. Funding, credentials and configuration still apply.

**Layer-confirmation bars.** The shortest listed duration receives 5 and the longest receives 1. Intermediate values use `5 − 4 × (duration − shortest) ÷ (longest − shortest)`, rounded to one decimal. If all listed durations are identical, each receives 5. The resulting indices are Atto 5, Nano 4.6, Base 4.7, Solana 4.4 and Lightning 1. Different timing boundaries and assurances mean these are source-specific observations rather than a controlled network ranking.

**Price vs ATH.** Native-asset scores use `1 + 4 × (current USD price ÷ recorded USD all-time high)`, capped at 5 and rounded to one decimal. Stablecoins receive 5 by convention. This measures price position, not dollar stability, volatility or safety.

The [CoinGecko API snapshot](https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3/coins/markets?vs_currency=usd&ids=atto,nano,bitcoin&sparkline=false) was fetched on November 2, 2026 at 09:59 UTC:

- ATTO: $0.00003932 versus a $0.00021937 ATH; provider update 02:50 UTC that day.
- XNO: $0.319043 versus a $33.69 ATH; provider update 09:58 UTC.
- BTC: $78,023 versus a $126,080 ATH; provider update 09:58 UTC.

ATTO's provider observation was about seven hours old at retrieval; the other two were about a minute old. These are dated inputs, not automatically updating quotes.

This excludes earlier wallet/client submission and is not Send-only. This is historical L2 preconfirmation, not full sealed-L2 inclusion, irrevocable finality or a complete CDP request. The published aggregate has no individual timings or measurement harness. - Solana: 399.254 ms mean across 402,618 observations in OpenChainBench's 24-hour window as of November 2 at 10:09 UTC. The collector times first slot visibility until a root notification covers it. This excludes earlier submission and CDP processing. The traffic was predominantly Cash App; exact timer events and raw records are undisclosed. This is provider-reported off-chain settlement rather than L402 API delivery. [Report](https://voltage.cloud/blog/cheapest-way-to-settle-payments-instantly-a-cost-first-comparison), [pilot announcement](https://sbcevents.com/events/voltage-sbc-summit-americas/).

The [Atto, Nano and RSNano benchmark repository](https://github.com/rotilho/feeless-benchmarks) contains reproduction commands and raw data for controlled single-host, one-voter tests. Those tests are separate from the mainnet observations above.

**Implementation notes.** A wallet tool, a payment network and a paid-HTTP protocol handle different steps. [x402](https://docs.x402.org/core-concepts/http-402) requests payment through HTTP 402; [L402](https://docs.lightning.engineering/the-lightning-network/l402/l402) uses a Lightning invoice and payment proof. For Atto/Nano, match the confirmed send's recipient, amount and purchase before delivery; Receive only makes funds spendable. Check existing payment status after a timeout before starting another payment.

Atto's CLI/MCP share [public service defaults](https://github.com/attocash/integrations/blob/main/atto-cli/src/wallet/defaults.ts). Their limits constrain that wallet profile, not arbitrary programs with unrestricted computer access. [n8n signing credentials](/docs/integration/n8n#enable-signing-only-where-it-is-needed) do not inherit those limits. [Commons](/build/commons) supports custom applications. Nano's [x402nano helper](https://github.com/x402nano/helper) needs an RPC URL; its [production integration guide](https://docs.nano.org/integration-guides/) covers key management rather than relying on the internal node wallet.

Direct Base transfers have [execution and publication fees](https://docs.base.org/specifications/transactions/network-fees); CDP includes settlement gas in its own charge. CDP batching can change per-call economics, but its EVM batch scheme needs a separate supported integration because `createX402Server` does not register it. [Current support](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/x402/support/faq).

Solana's [fee payer](https://solana.com/docs/payments/how-payments-work) can differ from the sender. A new [token account](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/basics/create-token-account) requires a refundable storage deposit; on [closure](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/basics/close-account), the owner or close authority selects its destination. Lightning self-hosting requires sufficient outbound/inbound [liquidity](https://docs.lightning.engineering/the-lightning-network/liquidity/how-to-get-inbound-capacity-on-the-lightning-network); [Aperture](https://docs.lightning.engineering/lightning-network-tools/aperture) supplies the L402 proxy path.
